Capuns, and the most correct grandmotherCapuns, and the most correct grandmother

Our head cook's recipe for the Graubünden classic, and the wine she insists you drink with it.

Seraina Tönz, head cook · 14 October 2026 · 5 min read

Every grandmother in Graubünden makes capuns, and every grandmother makes them correctly. That is the first thing our head cook, Seraina, tells new staff in the Arvenstube kitchen. The second thing is that ours are made the way her grandmother made them in Zuoz, and that this is the most correct of all.

What capuns are

Capuns are small parcels of spätzli dough, studded with air-dried beef and herbs, wrapped in a leaf of chard and simmered in milk and stock. They are then finished under a blanket of mountain cheese and brown butter. They are winter food, farmhouse food, and the most requested dish on our menu in every season.

Seraina's capuns (serves four)

For the dough: 250 g flour, 3 eggs, 100 ml milk, a pinch of salt, a handful of chopped parsley and chives, 60 g air-dried Grisons beef in small dice, 30 g salsiz in small dice.

To finish: 20 large Swiss chard leaves, 400 ml milk, 400 ml beef stock, 80 g grated Alpine cheese, 50 g butter.

  1. Beat the flour, eggs, milk and salt to a thick, elastic dough. Rest it for thirty minutes, then fold in the herbs and meats.
  2. Blanch the chard leaves for ten seconds, cool them in iced water and lay them flat.
  3. Place a tablespoon of dough on each leaf and roll it into a neat parcel.
  4. Lay the capuns in a wide pan, cover with the milk and stock, and simmer gently for twenty minutes.
  5. Lift into a dish, scatter with cheese and pour over butter that you have browned until it smells of hazelnuts.

Every grandmother makes them correctly. Ours are simply the most correct.

What to drink

Seraina insists on a glass of Completer, the old white grape of the Bündner Herrschaft — rare, slightly bitter, perfect against the butter. If you ask nicely, she might also tell you where the salsiz comes from. She has never told us.
