A winter of black iceA winter of black ice

Every few years the Engadin lakes freeze clear before the snow. Here is what happens next — and how to skate it safely.

Gian Andri Clalüna, mountain guide · 2 November 2026 · 4 min read

Most winters, the lakes of the Upper Engadin freeze under snow and stay white until April. Every few years something rarer happens: a hard cold snap arrives before the first big snowfall, and the lakes freeze clear. Locals call it black ice. For two or three weeks, you can skate across Lake Sils and look down through twenty centimetres of glass at the stones on the bottom.

How to know when it's safe

Nobody skates on black ice alone, and nobody skates it without someone who knows the lake. Our guide, Gian Andri, has measured the ice on Lake Sils every morning for twenty-two winters. He drills a hole, reads the thickness, listens.

The ice sings when it is thickening. It is the most beautiful sound in the valley, and it means you can go out.

The rule is simple: twelve centimetres of clear ice for walking, fifteen for skating in a group. The Gemeinde posts official clearance at the lakeshore; we never go out before it does.

What to bring

  • Nordic touring skates if you have them; we lend a full set otherwise.
  • Ice claws worn around the neck, a throw rope and a backpack with a dry change of clothes — all provided by the guide.
  • Something warm in a flask. We pack hot apple juice with cinnamon and a slice of nusstorte.

When it doesn't happen

Some winters, black ice simply doesn't come. The snow arrives early and the lakes go white. Then the same lakes become the ground for the Engadin Ski Marathon, 42 kilometres from Maloja to S-chanf, and for the polo and horse races on Lake St. Moritz. A frozen lake in the Engadin is never wasted.

If black ice does form during your stay, reception will tell you at breakfast. It is the only thing we will ever wake you early for.
